🌡️ How The SIH26083 Platform Works

Traditional heat warnings rely solely on dry-bulb air temperature. This platform fuses multi-source meteorological reanalysis with biometeorological human thermal stress and demographic vulnerability.

1. Biometeorological Hazard Indices (60% Weight)

  • Outdoor WBGT (ISO 7243): Accounts for direct solar radiation, humidity, and wind cooling. When WBGT exceeds 32°C, human metabolic heat dissipation fails, necessitating mandatory cessation of physical labor.
  • NOAA Heat Index: Apparent temperature combining humidity and air temp using the Rothfusz regression equation.
  • Bröde UTCI: Universal Thermal Climate Index calculating physiological heat balance in outdoor environments.

2. Ward-Level Heat Vulnerability Index (HVI) (40% Weight)

Synthesized from Census 2011 and PLFS micro-data across 5 vulnerability vectors:

  • Elderly Population (60+): Physiological thermoregulatory fragility.
  • Outdoor Workers: Daily-wage street vendors, construction, delivery personnel.
  • Informal / Slum Dwellings: Metal/tin roofs exacerbating Urban Heat Island (UHI).
  • Vegetation Canopy (NDVI): Inverse cooling buffer from urban green cover.
  • Hospital Bed Density: Access to emergency acute heatstroke care.

3. Historical Empirical Validation (May 2010 Landmark Heatwave)

Validated against the catastrophic 2010 Ahmedabad heatwave (documented in Azhar et al. 2014, PLOS ONE). The system successfully triggered Code Red Extreme Hazard 48 hours in advance, accurately predicting the window of 1,344 excess deaths.

📊 Traceable Data Sources & Provenance

Every number and advisory displayed in the Human Impact Card is grounded in verifiable public records. Nothing is fabricated.

1. Official Census & Survey Provenance

  • Primary Source: https://censusindia.gov.in / MoSPI Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)
  • Extraction / Standardization Date: 2026-09-10
  • Directly Measured Features: Municipal ward total resident population, senior citizens percentage (60+), informal labor percentage, and slum dwelling household percentage from the Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation (AMC) and Census 2011.

2. "Measured" vs "Derived" Cohort Accounting

  • Total Ward Population: Measured from AMC official administrative census records.
  • Children (Age 0–5): Derived at 9.2% benchmark (Census 2011 urban child ratio).
  • Youth (Age 6–17): Derived at 18.8% benchmark (Census 2011 urban youth ratio).
  • Working Adults (Age 18–59): Derived from total population minus dependent cohorts.
  • Outdoor vs Indoor Labor: Derived using 45% workforce participation rate (PLFS urban India) minus outdoor informal manual labor.

3. Clinical & Epidemiological Citations

  • WHO/WMO 2015: Heatwaves and Health: Guidance on Warning-System Development (WMO-No. 1142).
  • WHO 2021: Public health advice on heat: guidance for clinical and public health practitioners.
  • Azhar et al. 2014: Heat-Related Mortality in India: Excess All-Cause Mortality Associated with the 2010 Ahmedabad Heat Wave. PLOS ONE, 9(3): e91831.
  • NRDC & AMC 2018: Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan (HAP): Guide to Extreme Heat Planning.
  • NDMA 2019/2024: National Guidelines for Preparation of Action Plan - Prevention and Management of Heat Wave.
